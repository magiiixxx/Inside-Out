# BahayFind — Session 1 Pitch

Speakers: **A** opens and closes, **B** takes the middle. Swap the
labels if you have a different number of presenters.

## The script

**[A — The person]**
Meet Richelle. She is a student from the province, new to Iloilo, and
she needed a room near PHINMA UI. We watched her search. She messaged
more than eight owners on Facebook, asking the same two questions every
time: how much, and is it still free? Then she travelled to see a room
that looked perfect. It was already taken. The post was still up.

**[B — Today]**
That trip cost her about nine hundred pesos and more than six hours. It
happens every enrollment period, when hundreds of new students arrive at
once. Facebook shows posts. It does not show whether a room is still
free, or how old the post is. So students guess, and they pay for the
guess.

**[B — Our fix]**
We built BahayFind. Owners post their own rooms with a photo, the price,
the address, the distance from PHINMA UI, and the label that matters
most: available or occupied. Our team checks every posting before
students see it, and a room nobody updates for seven days disappears. A
student opens the site, with no login, sees what is really free today,
and calls the owner. Students who save a room can rate it, and anyone
with an account can report a room that turned out to be taken.

**[A — The demo moment]**
In Session 3 we will show it live on a phone. An owner signs up and
posts a room with a photo. We approve it. A student finds it under their
budget, sees that it is available, saves it, and calls. Three minutes.

**[A — How we'll know]**
Before Session 3, three people try it. Richelle will tell us whether she
would have skipped that nine hundred peso trip. Joshua, a student, will
find a room under his budget in under a minute. Monica, an owner, will
post her own room. Then we will tell you exactly what they said, good or
bad.

**[B — How we'll earn]**
Students use BahayFind for free. Owners start free with one room. An
owner with more rooms can ask for Premium, one hundred forty-nine pesos
a month, and pays our team directly by GCash, Maya or bank. The app
itself handles no money. We have not proven that anyone will pay. That
is the next thing we will test.

## The three hardest questions

**1. "You have watched one student. How do you know this is a real problem
for many?"**
This is the weakest point in our evidence. We watched Richelle, and her
story is concrete, but it is one person. Honest answer: "Richelle is our
clearest case. Before Session 3, Joshua and at least two more students
will tell us whether the same thing happened to them." Strengthen it now:
ask five students at PHINMA UI "Have you ever travelled to a room that
was already taken?" and report how many said yes.

**2. "Why would owners keep updating the label? If they don't, it is the
same as Facebook."**
This is the biggest risk in the design, because the whole promise depends
on owners updating their rooms. Honest answer: "Owners want full rooms,
and the label is one tap. We have not proven they will do it, which is
why Monica is testing it. If an owner does nothing, the room is hidden
after seven days until they renew it, and a student can report a room
that is already taken, which sends it to our team. A stale post cannot
stay up the way Richelle's did."

**3. "Why should a student trust 'approved' and the star ratings?"**
Honest answer: "Before a listing goes live, our team checks the owner's
permit number and their answers to three validation questions: are you
the owner or a caretaker, how many beds are open, and can we call this
number to confirm. That is all 'approved' means, so we say exactly that
and no more. The stars are new, so most rooms have none yet. Only a
student who saved a room can rate it, but we cannot prove they visited.
We can reset a room's ratings, and we will say so if asked." Be ready for:
"Do owners know their phone number and address will be public?" Yes, they
tick a box saying so when they post.

## One more question to expect

**"How will this make money, and does the premium plan work?"**
Honest answer: "Students stay free. Owners are the customers. Today a
Free owner can post one room. Premium and Property Manager owners can
post more, and that room limit is the only thing that differs so far.
Owners ask for a plan in the app, pay our team directly, and a team
member switches it on. The prices, ₱149 and ₱299 a month, are guesses.
We will ask real owners like Monica whether they would pay." The other
plan features are listed as "Coming soon" in the app and in
`docs/LATER.md`. Do not say they exist. Property Manager has no extra
features yet.

## Another question to expect

**"Can students reserve a room?"**
Honest answer: "Not in the app. A student saves a room, then calls the owner to
see it and arrange everything in person. We chose that for now because a
reservation needs deposits and holds, and the app handles no money. The
available or occupied label is how a student knows a room is still free." Do not
say students can book or reserve.

## Before you present

- Evidence is thin: one watched student, and the other two testers have
  not tried it yet. Do the five-student question above this week.
- Get at least one real owner (Monica) to agree to post, and say how many
  others you have asked.
- Before the demo, delete the SAMPLE rooms and the test rooms, and make a
  fresh owner and student account so the demo starts clean. Have the live
  address open on a phone.
- If you show Google sign-in, test it the same day: a Google app only
  works for the public once its consent screen is published.
- Practise it aloud once with a timer. If it runs over three minutes,
  cut the "Today" beat first.
